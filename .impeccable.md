# Design Context — Posture

## Target audience

People who sit at a Mac all day with AirPods already in — developers, designers,
anyone desk-bound. Technically literate enough to run a build script. Reykjavík
hobby project, not a funded product.

## Use cases

1. Install it, calibrate once, forget it exists until it taps you on the shoulder.
2. Glance at the menu bar to see whether the last hour was upright or hunched.
3. Tune how insistent it is, because tolerance for being nagged varies wildly.

## Brand personality / tone

**Minimal, clean, elegant** — stated directly by the creator, and the app itself
follows it: one saturated colour (a green ring), everything else chrome.

Honest over salesy. This is free, it's for fun, and it competes with five apps
that all promise to fix your posture. The tone that fits is dry and factual —
say what it does, admit what it can't, don't oversell. Faint humour is welcome;
enthusiasm is not.

Not: startup-launch energy, wellness-brand softness, productivity-guru urgency.

## What makes it different

Every competitor detects a slouch and alerts. None of them model what happens
when you *ignore* the alert. Posture backs off exponentially — each ignored
nudge doubles the wait, sitting up resets it. That is the idea worth leading
with.

## Honest constraints to communicate

- Reads head pitch, not spine. It's a nudge, not a clinical measure.
- Needs motion-capable AirPods (Pro, 3rd gen+, Max, Beats Fit Pro).
- Apple Silicon, macOS 14+.
- Ad-hoc signed: build it yourself, no notarized download.
